Project purpose and success criteria

Define what a successful programmable light should achieve using the class-agreed design, function, construction/build quality, electronics/programming and aesthetics criteria. Add one personal goal.

Evidence checklist
  • Short project explanation
  • All five class-agreed criteria categories
  • One personal goal

Tools, materials and components

Explain the normal purpose and possible project contribution of each confirmed item without inventing the actual use, sequence or setting.

Confirmed source list
  • Tenon saw, tri-square, pencil, steel rule, soldering iron, helping hands and solder
  • Radiata pine, wood screws and 3 mm clear acrylic
  • Arduino Uno, RGB LED strip, jumper wires, USB cable and ULS 4.0/4.6 laser cutter

Workshop safety and risk control

Choose the most serious identified hazard and explain the possible harm, the control and the action you would take if something became unsafe.

Teacher-controlled checks
  • Demonstration, safety-test and machine-permission status
  • Current local procedure and required PPE
  • First-aid and emergency-stop locations are Teacher to confirm

Read the Programmable Lamp drawing

Record the title block and scales, identify the views, transcribe each labelled dimension with its feature and view, explain the three cut labels in order, and list what still needs checking.

Plan-reading boundary
  • Use the full PDF, not a measured screen preview.
  • Keep the unit blank if the drawing does not state it.
  • Materials, tolerances and assembly details remain Teacher to confirm.

Two-layer vector-design practice

Explain how you separated the key-tag practice into a base layer and detail layer, recorded the exported filename, and what you will apply to later design work. The maximum 80 mm × 50 mm size applies only to the key-tag practice; it is not a final lamp dimension.

Practice-only boundary
  • The maximum 80 mm × 50 mm size applies only to the key-tag practice.
  • It is not a final lamp dimension.
  • Export, Classroom and laser requirements are Teacher to confirm.

Visual research with source attribution

Identify a useful shape, line, colour or lighting feature, explain how it could influence your work without copying, and record the creator, site, URL and reuse note.

Ethical research check
  • Record the image resolution.
  • Distinguish research inspiration from permission to publish.
  • Approved imagery remains Teacher to confirm.

Six lamp ideas

Record six numbered ideas. Explain what each idea explores and how it differs; any proposed colour or lighting effect is an idea, not a supplied technical requirement.

Variation prompts
  • This idea explores…
  • It differs from the others because…
  • The light could…

Selected design and justification

Compare ideas against the class-agreed criteria, justify the strongest direction, record one refinement and identify one unresolved feasibility question.

Decision boundary
  • Refer to evidence from research and the six ideas.
  • Separate a confirmed criterion from a preference.
  • Feasibility approval and production constraints are Teacher to confirm.

Materials and sustainability decision

Use timber-production and sustainability evidence to justify a material decision. Explain the confirmed role of 3 mm clear acrylic without adding unsupported properties.

Evidence checklist
  • Hardwood/softwood and native-forest/plantation comparisons
  • Ordered timber-production sequence
  • Supplier, stock size, grade, finish and tolerance are Teacher to confirm.

Algorithm and digital-system plan

Use coding terms, input-process-output thinking and a flowchart or pseudocode to describe intended light behaviour and one observable test case.

Teacher-controlled details
  • Wiring, pins, LED-strip type, library and electrical requirements
  • Final code and upload procedure
  • Record only authorised connections and code.

Production, problem-solving and test record

Keep a dated action/result log, identify the safety check, and record a problem, response, observed test result and retest decision.

Evidence boundary
  • Use the supplied plan, theory or a named teacher checkpoint.
  • Do not fabricate a source or unobserved result.
  • Practical order, test values and sign-off points are Teacher to confirm.

Final evaluation and evidence hand-in

Judge the completed solution against each agreed criterion using evidence from earlier cards. Identify a strength, limitation, realistic improvement and sustainability learning.

Submission boundary
  • Cite the evidence card that supports each judgement.
  • Final photo, code, filename and submission route are Teacher to confirm.
  • Browser autosave is preparation, not submission.
