Teacher-developed integration frame

Five-module scope and sequence

Important: Website Weeks 1–10 organise learning access only. They do not create or authorise a practical production sequence.
Module 1 · Weeks 1–2

Brief, safety and project information

Week 01 and Week 02 source material exists

Learning focus

  • Explain how physical design, named materials/components, electronics and code can contribute to a complete solution.
  • Distinguish hazard, risk and control; identify the conditions that precede teacher-authorised machine use.
  • Use written dimensions and scale information carefully; record uncertainty rather than guessing.

Named theory sections

  1. 1.1 What makes a programmable lamp?
  2. 1.2 Workshop safety: hazards, risk controls and responsibility
  3. 1.3 Measurement, scale and reading the supplied drawing

Source-grounded content

  • Unit introduction and class-agreed success criteria
  • Named tool, material and component identification
  • Workshop hazard/risk/control reasoning
  • Fixed disc-sander SOP dependency and access controls
  • Measurement conversions, scale and supplied drawing interpretation

Evidence available

  • 30 knowledge checks
  • 6 scaffolded written responses
  • Folio Cards 01, 02, 03, 04
  • Disc Sander Safety Quiz result; pass rule, attempts and record location are Teacher to confirm

TE4-MSC-01, TE4-PPM-01, TE4-DES-01, TE4-SAF-01, TE4-DIG-02

Module 2 · Weeks 3–4

Measurement, vector design and ideas

Week 03 practice exists; Week 04 folder is empty

Learning focus

  • Judge image resolution and design relevance while recording source and reuse information.
  • Organise a simple vector practice design using outlines and two layers.
  • Generate six ideas, compare them and justify a direction against class-agreed criteria.

Named theory sections

  1. 2.1 Image quality, resolution and design research
  2. 2.2 Vector design: outlines, layers and laser-ready simplicity
  3. 2.3 Iterating ideas and building success criteria

Source-grounded content

  • Two-layer key-tag vector practice
  • Image resolution and source/reuse record
  • Six distinct lamp ideas
  • Observable criteria and source-aware design justification

Evidence available

  • 30 knowledge checks
  • 6 scaffolded written responses
  • Folio Cards 05, 06, 07, 08
  • Week 03 source directs export/upload to Google Classroom; exact route, export format and naming are Teacher to confirm

Module 3 · Weeks 5–6

Materials, production and sustainability

Week 05 and Week 06 folders are empty; separate timber theory exists

Learning focus

  • Distinguish hardwood/softwood classification from assumptions about physical hardness.
  • Compare native forests and plantations using evidence rather than declaring one automatically better.
  • Connect drawing information, material reasoning and an evidence plan while preserving missing production detail.

Named theory sections

  1. 3.1 Hardwood, softwood and a reasoned material choice
  2. 3.2 Native forests, plantations and sustainable choices
  3. 3.3 Planning timber production from drawing to evidence

Source-grounded content

  • Timber groups and source-bounded material reasoning
  • Native forest and plantation comparison
  • Sustainability reasoning with evidence limitations
  • Cautious evidence planning linked to the supplied drawing and teacher checkpoints

Evidence available

  • 30 knowledge checks
  • 6 scaffolded written responses
  • Folio Cards 09

Module 4 · Weeks 7–8

Coding and digital systems

Week 07 and Week 08 folders are empty; separate coding theory exists

Learning focus

  • Explain algorithms, sequencing, syntax, variables, loops and conditionals using general language.
  • Describe an input/process/output or controller/output relationship at a conceptual level.
  • Plan intended behaviour and an observable test case without fabricating hardware or code.

Named theory sections

  1. 4.1 Coding language: algorithms, sequencing and syntax
  2. 4.2 Variables, loops and conditionals for light behaviour
  3. 4.3 Connecting code, controller and light as a system

Source-grounded content

  • General coding terminology
  • Conceptual algorithms, pseudocode, flowcharts or block diagrams
  • Instructions/controller/light-output system reasoning
  • Observable test-case planning

Evidence available

  • 30 knowledge checks
  • 6 scaffolded written responses
  • Folio Cards 10
  • Any code screenshot or system test must come from teacher-authorised work and record the actual source/configuration

Before practical delivery

Safety and dependency boundaries

This public page grants no equipment permission and supplies no local safe-work procedure.
  1. The website and program do not grant permission to use equipment.
  2. Current school WHS procedures, ESIS, teacher demonstrations, training and supervision override website wording.
  3. Fixed disc-sander use requires current controls, teacher demonstration, required safety test, teacher permission and observed safe use; local PPE, emergency-stop and first-aid arrangements are Teacher to confirm.
  4. Laser-cutter workflow, file checking, authorised material, ventilation, supervision and settings are Teacher to confirm; the key-tag activity is software practice, not machine authorisation.
  5. Soldering/electronics work must not begin from this program alone; approved circuitry, ratings, power, connections and local safety procedure are Teacher to confirm.
  6. The supplied drawing must be opened at full size; a screen preview must not be measured, and unclear units/features must be referred to the teacher.
  7. Weeks 4–10 practical content is a documented dependency gap; teacher-approved job sequence/checkpoint records are required before practical delivery is represented.
  8. Student photos and researched images require current privacy, copyright, reuse and publishing decisions; public website access is not consent.
  9. The optional executable is excluded pending local security and curriculum approval.
